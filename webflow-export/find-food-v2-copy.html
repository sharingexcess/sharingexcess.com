There is no shortage of food in America. There is a shortage of access. Here is what that actually means.

The scale
40% of all food produced in America goes unsold or uneaten.

That is 140 billion pounds every single year. Grown by real farmers. Harvested by real workers. Never eaten by anyone. One farmer in California left 100 acres of perfectly ripe cantaloupes to decompose in the field because harvesting them would have cost more than he could sell them for. That story plays out across every state, every season.

The farmer's voice
The people who grow our food deserve to see it eaten.
"I am a farmer and rancher down in Texas, and the one thing that makes us madder than anything is that these incredibly nutritious items that we raise do not go to nourish our fellow man."
Mike Meyer, Farmer and Sharing Excess Partner

Mike represents every grower we work with across the country. Farmers who built their lives around feeding people, watching perfectly good food go nowhere because the economics made it impossible to do otherwise. A single farm feeds about 170 people per year. That work should not be for nothing.

The solution
We built the system to connect them.

Sharing Excess is a logistics platform. We find the surplus, move it fast, and get it to the 2,000+ community organizations that put food in front of families who need it. Every pound tracked. Every delivery documented.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the food really free?

Yes! Our food is 100% free. Because we receive surplus food from major distibutors, we are able to pass it onto the community at no-cost to you.

Do I need an ID to get food?

You will never be asked for an ID, a proof of residence or current immigration status. This food is free for everyone and anyone who needs it.

Where are your pop-ups?

We distribute free food at pop-ups and distribution locations in Philadelphia, New York, Chicago, Detroit, Los Angeles and beyond.

What kinds of food are at your distributions?

We offer all kinds of fresh produce. Including fruit like strawberries, apples, peaches, bananas and more. We also offer onions, potatos, squash and other fresh produce. All for free!

Where does your food come from?

 We partner with local organizations and businesses to bring rescued produce directly to the community! Our food is donated by major food companies including many brand names your familiar with. When they have extra food that can't make it to the grocery store in time, they give it to us to offer to those who need it.
